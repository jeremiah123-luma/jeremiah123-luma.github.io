Fitness Trackers for Camping: One Less Battery to Plan Around

Daytime activity, a night in the tent and one less charging cable to pack.

Andy
September 20, 2026
See Ray options

One less device competing for the power bank

On a camping trip, the phone, headlamp and camera all have their own battery needs. A tracker that can replenish itself from outdoor sunlight gives you one less dedicated charging cable to remember.

Ray is a small, screenless health tracker. It records everyday activity, steps, sleep, heart rate, HRV, temperature trends and light exposure, with the details in its companion app. Its seven-gram device keeps the weight on your wrist low; the band adds to the worn weight.

Check the battery before packing

Open the app before you leave and check the actual battery level. Ray can have up to about two months of reserve from a full charge at roughly five-minute sampling. More frequent sampling, enabled step counting and repeated Bluetooth reconnects shorten that reserve.

For a weekend trip, knowing the battery and settings is more useful than assuming you will find the perfect sunny campsite. For a longer trip, check both the charging plan and how much unsynced data your current firmware can store.

Give the solar panel an outdoor view

About one minute of direct sunlight per day can sustain operation, depending on exposure and use. A panel hidden under a sleeve or inside a sleeping bag cannot collect that light. Deep shade and changing weather affect the result, too.

Let Ray see suitable outdoor sunlight when convenient and watch the battery trend in the app. Ordinary indoor lighting is not a charging source to plan around. Read the current charging guidance before you leave.

A tracker has to be comfortable in a sleeping bag

The band you barely notice around camp can feel different with your wrist under a pillow. Try sleeping in your chosen strap at home first. Check the underside of the clasp, keep the fit comfortable and follow the care instructions for sweat, water and cleaning.

Ray has no wrist display to light up the tent. If you mainly review your sleep afterward, that can be a useful trade-off. Our sleep-tracker comfort guide covers the fit details.

Plan the phone connection as well as the battery

Set up the app and sync before departure. If you expect to spend long periods away from your phone, confirm the supported storage duration and reconnection steps for your firmware. A long battery reserve does not mean unlimited storage for unsynced readings.

Ray's role is everyday health tracking around your trip. Keep maps, route recording and emergency communication with the equipment you use for those jobs.

Going for a longer trail day? Read fitness trackers for hiking. For daily steps around camp or at home, start with how Ray tracks outdoor walks.
