Low-EMF Fitness Trackers: What Bluetooth-Off Actually Means

A Bluetooth-off option gives you control over a tracker’s wireless connection.

Andy
September 9, 2026
See Ray options

Three settings that matter more than a label

If you are shopping for a low-EMF fitness tracker, look past the headline and check its actual wireless controls. Can you disable Bluetooth on the tracker? Can you choose when to sync? Can you change how often it transmits?

Chargerless Ray supports all three. These are practical choices about how your device communicates, rather than a promise about your health.

Bluetooth off and phone Bluetooth off are different

Turning Bluetooth off on your phone does not necessarily turn off the radio inside a nearby tracker. A device may continue advertising that it is available to connect. If your intention is to stop the tracker’s Bluetooth communication, use its supported device-level control.

Ray supports disabling Bluetooth. Reconnecting is necessary before new readings can reach the app over Bluetooth. The syncing help page covers normal connected operation; ask us for the current steps for changing radio settings on your version of Ray.

Transmit power and duty cycle answer different questions

Transmit power describes the radio’s output while it is sending a signal. Duty cycle describes how much of the time it spends doing that. A radio can transmit briefly at one power setting, or more frequently at another.

Ray uses low transmit power and a low duty cycle as part of its energy-efficient design. You can also adjust the transmission interval. The resulting wireless activity depends on how you configure and use the device.

Power use and charging matter too

Radio on-time is the biggest factor, but not the only one. A device that draws more power tends to produce more field while it runs, and the way it charges matters: wireless (inductive) charging creates a strong field, and trackers you keep wearing while they charge put that close to your skin. Charging with the device off your wrist matters much less.

Ray keeps all three low. It charges from light as direct current, with no wireless coil. It is screenless and draws very little power. And its Bluetooth transmits in short intervals, or stays off until you sync. Screenless does not mean wireless-free, though: Ray uses Bluetooth Low Energy when it syncs.

Build the routine around when you read your data

If you usually review sleep in the morning, an on-demand sync may fit your habits. If you want fresh readings in the app throughout the day, a connected configuration may be more convenient. Choose the behavior that matches your preferences, and confirm storage limits before leaving any tracker disconnected for a long period.
