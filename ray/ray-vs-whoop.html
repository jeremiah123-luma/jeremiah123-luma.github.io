Ray versus WHOOP 5.0

Both are screen-free bands. The difference is WHOOP's coaching membership versus Ray's no-subscription, light-powered design.

Andy
Fact-checked September 7, 2026 · 38 independent comparisons
See Ray options from $219

WHOOP 5.0 is a screen-free performance band built around strain, recovery and sleep coaching. You don't buy the band outright: it comes with an annual membership that currently runs $199 to $359 a year. Ray is also screen-free, but it's a 7-gram band you buy once, with no membership, that powers itself from light instead of a charger.

The short answer

Choose WHOOP if its strain and recovery coaching is the reason you want a tracker, and the yearly membership is worth it to you. Choose Ray if you want everyday sleep and health tracking in a lighter band, with no subscription and no charging routine.

WHOOP 5.0 status: Currently sold.

Common questions

Does WHOOP 5.0 require a membership?

Yes. US annual list prices are $199 for One and $239 for Peak with WHOOP 5.0, or $359 for Life with WHOOP MG.

Does Ray require a membership?

No. Ray's core app and tracking do not require a recurring plan.

Do both trackers work without a screen?

Yes. Both keep the wearable itself screen-free and put detailed results in a phone app.

Is Ray the same as WHOOP's strain and recovery coaching?

No. Both use signals such as heart rate and HRV, but WHOOP has its own mature strain, sleep and recovery system; the scores should not be treated as interchangeable.
