Can You Turn Off Bluetooth on a Fitness Tracker? Here’s How Ray Works

Yes. Ray lets you turn off Bluetooth on the tracker and sync on demand.

Andy
September 23, 2026
See Ray options

Turn it off on the tracker, not just your phone

Turning Bluetooth off on your phone stops the phone from connecting. It does not necessarily stop a fitness tracker from advertising its own signal. If you want the tracker’s radio off, look for a device-level control.

Ray supports disabling Bluetooth on the device, on-demand syncing and an adjustable transmission interval. Those are separate options: switching the radio off is different from leaving it on and having it transmit less often.

Will Ray keep tracking with Bluetooth off?

The tracker collects readings on the device; turning off the Bluetooth connection means the app cannot receive fresh data until you reconnect and sync. Do not treat that as unlimited offline storage. Confirm the supported storage period and reconnection steps for your firmware if you plan to leave Ray disconnected for a long time.

If you need live readings on your phone throughout the day, keep Bluetooth available. If you mainly review sleep and activity later, an intentional sync routine may fit better.

A simple on-demand sync routine

Pick a time when you normally look at your data, such as after waking up. Enable the connection, sync and check that the app has your latest readings. Then choose the Bluetooth setting you want for the rest of the day. The exact controls can vary with app and firmware version; ask Chargerless for the current steps rather than following instructions for a different tracker.

What this does—and does not—mean for EMF

With Ray’s Bluetooth switched off, it is not transmitting over that connection, and radio on-time is the biggest factor in a tracker’s EMF. Ray also charges from light as direct current (no wireless charging coil) and uses very little power, which keeps the rest low. When it syncs it uses Bluetooth Low Energy, so “radiation-free” would still be misleading.

For the broader buying question, read our low-EMF fitness tracker guide. For the difference between transmit power, transmission interval and duty cycle, see the Bluetooth and low-EMF explainer.
